# Devpost written narrative — Kira FounderOps

**Category: Small Business Services**

Kira FounderOps is an AI-native operating brief for solo founders and very small businesses. It was created from a problem I experience directly while building Kira Labs: important decisions, deadlines, outreach ideas, event commitments, hardware constraints, product claims, and open questions become scattered across notes, emails, and conversations. A large company can assign people to operations, project management, communications, and risk review. A solo founder usually cannot. The result is lost continuity, duplicated work, avoidable mistakes, and founder overload.

Kira FounderOps uses the Gemini API to convert messy founder input into a structured operating brief. The founder supplies the business name, current objective, notes, known metrics, and real customer or user signals. Gemini then produces an executive summary, evidence-backed decisions, risks with mitigations, prioritized next actions, an outreach draft, and a list of unknowns. The prompt explicitly prohibits invented customers, revenue, metrics, dates, and commitments. Each decision contains an evidence field, and missing information is surfaced rather than silently filled in.

AI is live in the core production path. Every operating brief triggers a real Gemini API request. The application requests structured JSON so the result can be rendered as an operational dashboard rather than an unstructured chat response. The system records the model version, response ID, token usage, timestamp, input hash, input, and output in an execution log. When deployed to Google Cloud Run, the same service can optionally write run and approval records to Cloud Firestore. This makes the AI work visible and auditable.

The human founder remains responsible for truth and final decisions. The AI performs synthesis, prioritization, risk identification, and drafting. The human chooses what information to provide, verifies the evidence, corrects mistakes, decides which recommendations to follow, and approves the action plan. The product includes a separate human-approval endpoint and log. Approval does not automatically send an email, spend money, change a file, or affect an outside system. This separation reflects the design principle behind Kira Labs: memory and AI recommendations should be reviewable, while external actions should be permissioned.

Kira FounderOps currently operates as an internal tool for Kira Labs, a founder-led AI research and product-development effort in Newark, New Jersey. Kira Labs is building Kira World, a local-first platform exploring persistent synthetic people with distinct identity, user-reviewable memory, voice, shared digital environments, and permissioned actions. FounderOps takes those continuity ideas and applies them to a narrow small-business service that can be tested today.

The current human role is one founder. The current AI role is an operations analyst and drafting assistant. No employees have been displaced. Instead, the system is intended to give resource-constrained founders access to a basic operating discipline that is normally available only to larger teams. If developed into a commercial service, it could create work for onboarding specialists, small-business coaches, integration developers, data-quality reviewers, support staff, and industry-specific template designers. It could also help existing small businesses spend less time reconstructing context and more time serving customers or hiring people.

The business-impact thesis is straightforward: small companies do not fail only because they lack ideas. They also fail because decisions and evidence are fragmented, deadlines are missed, and founders are overloaded. FounderOps can reduce that operational loss by creating a repeatable daily brief and a visible approval trail. A future paid version could offer subscriptions for solo founders, small project teams, nonprofit operators, and local service businesses, with optional connectors and organization-specific templates.

This submission is honest about its stage. Kira Labs has not generated product revenue from Kira FounderOps and does not yet have external paying customers. The P&L reports zero revenue if that remains true at submission time, and the repository contains a customer-evidence template that must only be filled with real contacts and real feedback. The working prototype, source code, live Gemini calls, Cloud Run deployment path, and execution logs are real. The next milestone is to place the tool in front of a small number of real founders, measure whether it saves time and prevents missed actions, and test a simple paid pilot.

Building the product this way reinforced an important lesson: an AI-native business should not hide uncertainty behind confident prose. Useful business agents need structured evidence, explicit unknowns, durable logs, and human approval. Kira FounderOps is a small demonstration of that operating model—a solo founder using Gemini not as a disposable chatbot, but as a visible and reviewable part of the business process.
