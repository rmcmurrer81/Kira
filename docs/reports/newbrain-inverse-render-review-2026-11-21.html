NewBrain · Experiment November 19 · Reviewed November 21, 2026

Inverse-render training did not qualify.

The predeclared object-first curriculum failed the frozen exact-answer comparison. A same-budget owner-first control performed better on the target inverse task. All ten strict qualification gates failed, and neither model was adopted.

What actually ran

Two independent small experimental Transformer arms restored the same complete step-22,800 parent, optimizer history, saved clocks and RNG. Each performed 2,048 actual local-GPU training updates to step 24,848, for 4,096 updates total. Six GPU processes completed. The grammar, order, position and token boundaries changed together; the study does not isolate a single causal mechanism.

Why the result failed

The object-first primary gained five reverse answers over its parent but scored ten fewer than control; the frozen requirement was at least eight more than each. Its 65/80 earlier-memory total masks eight lost individual answers previously correct in the parent. It improved inverse counter-mapping by only 1/8 and regressed forward counter-mapping to 4/8. The control's narrower inverse gain is not full qualification either.

An independent standard-library auditor rescored 2,304 raw records from six saved runs, checked 576 runtime-plan chunks, 73,728 runtime rows and 4,096 training collation hashes. Three cold replay pairs reproduced 384 raw outputs byte-exactly, including incorrect answers. These verify the stored experiment, not generalized learning.

Limits and future study

The 384 synthetic cases are consumed; some bindings and templates were familiar. No independently human-blind general-dialogue test or reliable retention is established. Proposed future work is a fresh, frozen bidirectional curriculum and retention comparison. No Qwen replacement, trained 9B model, safe companion memory, physical anatomy or adopted mind follows.
