Even supplying a correct oracle event did not guarantee correct output. That is an observed context-use limitation, but these comparisons cannot by themselves distinguish representational binding failure from context length, training distribution, formatting or decoding effects.

All 540 of 540 planned final restart answers reproduced in a distinct ordinary process with zero added updates. The saved states, parameters, counters and source identities matched. A separate earlier numerical precision diagnostic does not override these measured complete-answer results.

Study 2 · Six-world matched replay-form scheduling

Completed October 26. A different fixed experiment compared frozen, synchronized old-question scheduling and staggered scheduling across six fresh synthetic worlds. The two taught conditions used matched updates, row ordering and fact/form exposure; the change altered old-question form co-occurrence and phase together, so it cannot isolate a general diversity effect.

Both taught schedules retained 19 of the 26 previously correct old answers and lost the same seven. Synchronized replay recovered seven previously incorrect old answers; staggering recovered six. Therefore the single old-case difference is a missed recovery, not extra loss of a formerly correct case. New-fact and transfer success sets were identical across schedules. These results show no observed benefit from this specific intervention, not equivalence among all replay methods.

The experiment completed 36,864 SGD updates and 294,912 example presentations. A separate process reproduced all 324 of 324 planned final answers from 18 states, with unchanged saved state, parameters, counters and identities and no further updates. Total recorded free answers included 432 during learning and 324 at restart; read-only expected-path observations were performed separately after free generation.

Value selection, not missing EOS

Every wrong final answer first differed at the third, value-bearing token. All complete answers ended with true EOS. In synchronized replay, the correct value ranked second or third in 40 of 51 wrong known-fact cases; in staggered replay, 41 of 52. Across both schedules, the correct-value probability improved for all 48 new/transfer questions, but 14 new-association and 15 transfer answers were still wrong. Higher probability is not the same as a correctly selected complete answer.

A proposed next controlled test would hold a trained state, question form and other input tokens fixed while changing only the subject in fresh preregistered cases, then compare free output and value-logit distributions. This subject-sensitivity probe is unrun and would not by itself establish internal memory retrieval.

Evidence limitations and next step

Original12 unchanged: the accepted 2/12 result, earlier failed trained 0/12 candidate and unbound accepted checkpoint are separate from both successful small synthetic-reference studies. They cannot be treated as upgraded versions of the accepted learner.

All three studies used small, closed-vocabulary worlds; those worlds and seeds are consumed and must not be rerun or tuned. The six-world study was internally reviewed against saved journals, answer rows and source identities, not externally replicated across independent laboratories. Peak RAM and full physical I/O were not measured to a complete host-level standard in these studies. Unknown/abstention control behavior was not explicitly taught.

Later update: a separate six-world subject-sensitivity test and then a distinct NB14 value-weighted study were completed. A new varied-form teaching comparison is proposed only and remains unrun. Separate acquisition, context benefits and answer selection in future tests without changing accepted Original12.
