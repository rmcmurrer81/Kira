NewBrain: durable state, but meaning acquisition still fails

A November 1 opt-in native Adam candidate recorded exact optimizer moments/age, guarded zero-update adoption and a separate-process reload. This is persistence engineering, not improved answers. An optional score-normalized attention candidate then passed scoped source/math checks, including invented finite-difference fixtures. A subsequent separately measured continuing attention-policy study returned 0/6 direct acquisition, 0/6 paraphrases and 0/6 unfamiliar use, with 2/6 unsupported-question results, both immediately and after delayed restart.

On November 2, the isolated CONTENT_STATE_MEAN_DIRECT readout proposal averaged delivered content-position states rather than relying only on the terminal recurrent state. Its portable source review covered eleven groups and 37 negative cases; the ordinary test suite reported 79 passes and 34 optional skips from 113 tests. The proposed readout had not run a new independent learning qualification. Preserving the wrong answer across restart is not learning it correctly. The earlier protected Original12 result remains 2/12, not retested.

Outside research worth testing, not adopting blindly

GradMem (ICML 2026) optimizes small prefix memories with fixed main-model weights; compare a memory-only baseline to costly neural updates. FOREVER (ACL 2026) schedules replay by optimizer update magnitude rather than merely counting steps; test for old-control regression. Replay Can Provably Increase Forgetting (2026) cautions that careless replay can worsen retention; include a no-replay control. These papers test different models and datasets; none is evidence that NewBrain has acquired general language ability.

Outside reconstruction and anatomy references

SAM 3D Body (CVPR 2026) estimates body, hand and foot pose using a parametric body representation and optional 2D guidance; benchmark its silhouette/landmark consistency without copying its meshes. SOMA (March 2026 preprint) demonstrates adapters across incompatible human-body topologies and rigs; study a neutral common landmark vocabulary.

CADS (Nature Communications, October 28, 2026) describes 167 anatomical CT labels across 22,022 volumes. It is a segmentation reference, not a fully animated person, a patient-specific digital twin or biological simulation. CADS distribution offers model variants with different licenses; each training-data source has separate reuse terms. For Avatar Builder's original-only policy, start with independently authored anatomical geometry and use licensed datasets strictly for permitted validation and educational comparisons.
