The right answer from the wrong record is still wrong

These are reviewed, source-committed summaries of separate local experiments. The website review did not independently rerun model training. Real derivative training, read-only diagnostics, dataset checks and fabricated-score tests must not be combined or mistaken for acceptance.

1. Matched relationship-and-field training did not qualify

Four candidate derivatives completed 512 local CUDA optimizer updates total. The original Native decoder and the saved baseline readers were not retrained. Each reader’s held-out questions included 384 supported and 384 unsupported cases; earlier retention covered 4,032 saved replies.

All four candidates failed frozen gates despite the balanced arm’s higher counts. A separate, zero-update recombination of saved scores attributed the balanced arm’s 247 losses to field correction alone (188), relationship correction alone (56), and both corrections together (3). These are error-attribution counts, not additional learning.

2. Independent read-only source audit showed wrong-record copying

Two processes performed 80 local CUDA FP32 decoder forwards, with zero optimizer updates; raw outputs and features matched exactly after restart. The larger synthetic held audit included 1,920 supported and 1,408 expected-abstention cases per reader, with 4,032 historical retention comparisons.

The unchanged readers gave a right value from the wrong source on 160 and 125 supported cases. When the correct source changed but the answer text remained the same, both sources were correct on only 9/64 balanced and 2/64 replay-only role-switch pairs. Supplying true relationship and field labels in a separate post-hoc analysis helped diagnose the errors; it did not demonstrate independent recognition of those labels. Both calibrated endpoints still failed the retention veto.

3. Verified test inputs and fabricated objective are not learned performance

An independent byte readback verified all 5,504/5,504 assistant-authored synthetic source cases and their controlled edits. Six original tokenizer prompts exceeded the cap; after shortening a shared system instruction, the longest full held-out prompt occupied 311/320 tokens. File-access phase checks guarded fitting, calibration, inference and scoring separately. This work did not update the project model.

A proposed paired-intent objective passed nine fabricated unit tests. On fabricated scores, unary and paired CUDA training fixtures each reached 64/64 supported binding and 64/64 correct abstentions after 32 steps per optimizer (128 total). Both hit the fixture ceiling, giving no demonstrated comparative improvement; neither ran project-model forwards or updates. The real held-out paired-intent comparison remains unrun.

4. What the results do not establish

No new checkpoint, trained reader or selector was adopted. Reproducibility of the raw outputs includes reproducible mistakes. A correct value without the correct source cannot pass grounded answer qualification, and synthetic fixtures are not proof of robust question interpretation, continuing memory, dialogue, completed anatomy or embodiment. Accepted Original12 remains 2/12, not rerun. Private case tapes, prompts, checkpoints, candidate images and unlicensed reference media are withheld.
