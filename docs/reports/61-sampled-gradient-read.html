 HOW THE AUTOMATED TEST WORKS




 From a frozen plan
 to independently checked numbers
 1. Freeze the inputs and acceptance rules
 Before execution, the plan fixed three public synthetic examples, two probe states, twenty coordinates across
 ten parameter groups, two perturbation sizes, one deterministic direction and the rejection control. One probe
 used the inherited checkpoint; the other used an explicitly declared synthetic parameter recipe. These are
 diagnostic probes, not a new generalization benchmark.


 2. Check resources and run under controlled ownership
 Parallel work and the reminder were paused. Fresh launch checks used the existing 8 GiB available-memory
 startup guard and 3 GiB reserve. The startup sample showed about 13.4 GiB available. Controlled processes
 recorded their results, bounded outputs and cleanup evidence. The guard values were not lowered.


 3. Compare analytical and numerical derivatives
 For a parameter coordinate, the numerical estimate is:


   (loss(theta + h) - loss(theta - h)) / (2 x h)


 The two fixed h values were 1e-4 and 1e-5. The acceptance bound was absolute error <= max(1e-8, 0.001 x the
 larger derivative magnitude). Each coordinate was also checked for agreement across the two h values. A
 deterministic direction tested a combined change across the parameter vector.


 4. Require signal and deliberately test rejection
 Every parameter group had to meet the predeclared 1e-6 minimum signal. The tested Q, K and V maxima
 were about 5.04e-6, 9.68e-6 and 5.15e-6. An intentionally incorrect analytical gradient was then checked against
 both fixed perturbation sizes; both comparisons were rejected. This control reused saved measurements and
 added no model calls.


 5. Preserve originals and check the evidence separately
 The run recorded 170 objective evaluations. The independent saved-data audit checked 219 execution events,
 106 full raw data cells, 253 copied references, 19 one-time resource closes, two empty jobs and four complete
 output streams. The checking stage made no new model or execution-interface calls.

 INTERPRETATION / NEXT EXPERIMENT




 What this helps us
 investigate next
 The supported conclusion
 The sampled raw derivatives of the specified loss are numerically consistent under this fixed test. This
 makes an error in those sampled derivative calculations less likely as the explanation for the learning failures. It
 does not establish complete gradient coverage, optimizer correctness, independent reconstruction of the model
 state, useful new learning or unfamiliar-example generalization.


 Why the earlier record remains important
 The earlier numerical study remains inconclusive: its Q/K/V samples did not meet the fixed signal floor, and
 final evidence publication failed. The completed follow-up used a stronger, predeclared synthetic route gain to
 make the check informative. A separate launch attempt stopped on a metadata schema mismatch before model
 execution; that mismatch was corrected. The failed originals and their costs remain recorded.

 This acquisition-and-retention integration is in development and remains unrun. Evelyn's separate three-visitor memory test also
 remains unrun; saved conversation memory is distinct from changes to neural weights.

 COLLABORATION / NEXT RESEARCH




 Where a university can make a
 difference
 A concrete university collaboration
 The next useful partnership combines expertise in optimization, sequence generation, memory interference and
 experimental design. Lab computing and research assistants would support controlled repetitions, independent
 replication and careful analysis. More memory or a larger GPU would expand the experiments that can be
 attempted; equipment alone does not guarantee better learning.

    Research area                                  Useful contribution


    Learning and optimization                      Help design acquisition, retention, sequence-generation and forgetting
                                                   experiments with independent evaluation.


    Systems and computing                          Run controlled repetitions on university hardware, review resource use and
                                                   support reproducible research workflows.


    Human-computer interaction                     Evaluate Evelyn's conversation continuity, names/details across restarts,
                                                   visitor separation, voice and usability.


    Avatar and biomedical research                 Study measured deformation and anatomy. Validated organ physiology and
                                                   biological nerve-to-brain behavior remain future work.




 Evelyn and the avatar remain separate evidence tracks
 Evelyn's frozen 14-turn test involves three synthetic visitors, delayed recall, application restart, separation of
 visitor details and a corrected preference. It is unrun. Her head, voice and installer still require runtime testing
 and private user installation before public release. The existing body result is limited to 512 weighted skin
 vertices moving through five commanded poses and returning to their starting positions.


 Evidence and review scope
 The result was checked by separate internal roles and automated audits. External peer review and independent university
 replication remain pending. Current public status: NewBrain research record and KiraWorld research record. Detailed restricted
 evidence is retained locally for authorized technical review.
