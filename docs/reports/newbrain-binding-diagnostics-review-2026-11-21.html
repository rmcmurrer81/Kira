NewBrain · November 21, 2026 · Reviewed synthetic diagnostics

Reverse ownership binding remains unreliable.

Four local GPU diagnostic suites compared two unchanged NewBrain checkpoints. The tests did not train either model, qualify a replacement, or rerun Original12.

What actually ran

Four inference-only suites tested synthetic ownership facts using the same greedy full-precision (FP32) decoder. They compared the step-22,800 baseline with the previously rejected step-23,312 checkpoint. There were zero optimizer updates, zero replay steps and no model adoption during these tests. Frozen prompts, comparison outputs and runtime receipts were recorded privately; no private prompts, source or model files are published here.

For ownership-swap prompts, supported accuracy was 10/256 versus 24/256; reverse supported was 6/128 versus 17/128, and separate absent-label abstention was 5/32 versus 7/32. Each checkpoint reproduced archived raw token/UTF-8 outputs on all 80 previously tested prompts, including incorrect answers. An independent label oracle checked 288 fresh labels before decoding.

What these numbers do and do not establish

Reverse lookups remained weak for both checkpoints. The rejected checkpoint scored higher on some swap counts, but that is not an accepted learning improvement or an adoption decision. Cases within the same synthetic fact table are related; they should not be mistaken for independent human-language observations. Exact output replay establishes reproducibility, not reliable memory or generalization.

The November 19 matched training experiment remains a separate completed negative result: all ten strict gates failed, and neither trained arm was adopted. The accepted Original12 remains 2/12, not rerun.

Proposed next research

A future controlled design could train paired alternate bindings under equal update budgets, then test held-out ownership swaps, abstention and retention. This is only a proposal: no new training occurred in the November 21 diagnostics.

Publication and evidence limits

This reviewed public report summarizes private-source aggregate counts and experiment boundaries. It does not disclose source files, prompt contents, model weights, personal histories, reference media or restricted images. It is not an assessment of a production conversational assistant or of a biological, human or embodied person.
