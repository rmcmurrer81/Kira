Patient Horizon.A record that evolves with evidence.

Imagine a patient’s changing health information represented in a living, versioned research record—with an anatomical view, traceable findings, and a physician at the center of every decision.

That is Patient Horizon’s long-term research direction. Today, it begins with a small synthetic request-and-scenario planning prototype. Clinical monitoring, imaging interpretation, working organs and treatment recommendations are not operational.

Research plan · October 20, 2026

Patient Horizon: lung-focused demonstration

A proposed controlled demonstration would use one synthetic patient record, versioned results, a transparent anatomical display, timestamped bedside-data replay, and clearly labeled geometric breathing motion. It is planned, not a completed organ simulation.

Recorded engineering evidence is limited to synthetic request-planner checks and 512 weighted skin vertices moving through five commanded poses and returning to their starting references. Neither measurement proves physiological lung behavior, patient-specific treatment prediction, real-time hospital integration, or biological brain-to-lung coupling.

The next milestone needs frozen acceptance criteria, source and version history, invalid-data controls, numerical lung-model reference comparisons, and clinician review of any literature-based suggestions. NewBrain's separate model-learning assessments are not evidence of a validated medical function.

How a changing record could work

Evidence first.
Then the avatar.

The goal is a patient record that evolves when credible, authorized information arrives. A future anatomical display should follow documented evidence, not invent findings because a graphic looks realistic.

  1. 1Receive: validate source, patient authorization, dates, units and provenance.
  2. 2Version: preserve earlier labs, readings and images; identify corrections.
  3. 3Visualize: update only supported anatomical findings and show uncertainty.
  4. 4Review: present source-linked literature or follow-up questions for a qualified clinician.

This entire pipeline is a research proposal. It is not connected to health records and no automatic clinical recommendation engine exists today.

Connecting body, simulation and intelligence

A visible organ is not
a working organ.

Patient Horizon’s longer-term plan investigates three technologies that would need distinct verification: mathematical physiology, anatomical visualization, and an experimental cognitive interface.

Pulse is a candidate starting point for physiological modeling; Three.js could display approved anatomy; NewBrain is a separate learning-and-memory research track. None is currently linked to make a validated patient-specific lung or a two-way brain–body system.

An organ-level research path

Breathing requires
more than movement.

For an early synthetic-patient lung demonstration, Kira Labs would need defined scenarios and recorded outputs—not simply an animated mesh.

  1. 1Use approved lung and chest geometry to measure commanded motion and restoration.
  2. 2Model airway pressure, airflow, diaphragm movement and reference ranges under explicit assumptions.
  3. 3Separately validate gas-exchange and circulation calculations before making any physiological claims.
  4. 4Only later test any measured, bidirectional NewBrain input and output under disconnected controls.

The current research has not passed these lung or bidirectional interface milestones.
