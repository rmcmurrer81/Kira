NewBrain · October 26, 2026 · Verified three-world learning and restart

NewBrain's small test learner acquired new answers and transferred some question forms

In a separate fixed 39-token, 8,343-parameter experimental learner, complete new-fact answers improved from 0/12 frozen to 6/12 taught, with alternate-form transfer improving 0/12 → 4/12. Old answers went from 15/24 to 16/24, but four previously correct old answers were forgotten and five previously wrong ones recovered. All 540 final answers reproduced after a distinct-process restart with no additional learning.

Supplying additional retrieved context did not automatically improve performance: raw history scored 4/12 on new facts, and even a single correct oracle event scored 3/12 versus 6/12 without context. This does not isolate a specific neural mechanism. The separate accepted Original12 result is still 2/12, with the prior failed trained 0/12 preserved. Read complete results and limitations →.

NewBrain · October 26, 2026 · Verified six-world replay-form study

Changing old-question schedules did not improve new-fact or transfer accuracy

Across six fresh closed-vocabulary worlds, synchronized and staggered replay each produced 10/24 newly taught answers and 9/24 unfamiliar question forms. Old-answer totals were 26/48 and 25/48; both lost the same seven formerly correct answers. The difference is one less recovered old answer, not additional forgetting. All 324 final answers matched on restart.

Saved diagnostics locate all wrong final outputs at the value token; all outputs ended with true EOS. A new subject-sensitivity probe is proposed, not performed. This does not prove every replay method is equivalent or change accepted Original12. Read tables and next research question →.
