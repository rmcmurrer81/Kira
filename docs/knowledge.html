How dates are used

Video entries use their public publication date. A commit date records repository history; it does not automatically establish a lab founding date or a product launch. ShiftBrief’s release timestamp is shown in Eastern Time, with its UTC equivalent in the milestone.

Undated prototypes and active development retain a review date instead of an invented completion date. Future goals have no promised schedule. The timeline is a curated public record, not live monitoring.

Iris’s knowledge boundary

Iris is a browser-based automated guide that selects from reviewed public answers. No generative-model endpoint or API key is configured. She can explain the listed projects, basic installation and backup notes, public status, the lab timeline, and ways to contact Robert.

Unsupported questions receive an explicit handoff to Robert. Iris cannot access ShiftBrief records, private development, customer data or messages; make employment, legal, financial or partnership decisions; confirm unpublished dates; or speak as Kira.

Sarah remains the name used in the ShiftBrief product and Sarah Travel project. Iris is only the site guide. The original Sarah scripts and knowledge packs remain preserved in the repository; the new guide has its own curated public answers.
